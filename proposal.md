# AI and the Future of Work: Policy Proposal

## To:
Deputy Secretary, Employment & Skills

## Executive Summary
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming labour markets by reshaping job tasks, altering skill demand, and influencing wage distribution. While AI has the potential to increase productivity and economic growth, it also risks displacing workers and increasing inequality in the short term. This proposal evaluates three policy responses: reskilling programs, wage insurance schemes, and competition and data policy reform. 

It recommends a combined policy package that supports worker transition, promotes inclusive growth, and ensures competitive market structures. Implementation should occur over a 3–5 year period with clear performance indicators including employment transitions, wage recovery, and training participation rates. This approach ensures that AI adoption delivers long-term economic benefits while minimising social disruption.

## Problem Definition & Context
AI is not simply replacing jobs, but transforming the tasks that make up jobs. Advances in machine learning and automation technologies allow firms to substitute routine and predictable tasks while enhancing productivity in non-routine work.

The economic impact of AI operates through three key channels:

- **Automation effect**: AI replaces routine labour  
- **Augmentation effect**: AI enhances worker productivity  
- **Creation effect**: AI generates new jobs and industries  

The main policy challenge is the transition gap. Workers displaced from declining roles often lack the skills required for emerging jobs, leading to unemployment, underemployment, and wage pressure.
## Analysis of Impacts
### Employment and Tasks
AI is expected to reduce demand for routine and middle-skill jobs while increasing demand for high-skill and service-based roles. This leads to labour market polarisation, where employment grows at the top and bottom of the skill distribution.

### Wages and Inequality
AI increases the marginal productivity of high-skilled workers, leading to higher wages for these groups. In contrast, displaced workers may face wage declines, increasing income inequality.

### Industrial Structure
Firms with access to large datasets and advanced AI capabilities gain a competitive advantage, increasing market concentration. This raises concerns about reduced competition and growing monopoly power.

### Distributional Effects
The impact of AI is uneven:

- Lower-skilled workers face higher displacement risk  
- Urban regions benefit more than regional areas  
- Knowledge-intensive industries grow faster  

These effects create both economic opportunities and equity challenges.
